The rocket motor build-out, 2026

Missile production in the U.S. is limited by one part more than any other: the solid rocket motor. This report pulls together what companies and the Pentagon have said publicly in 2025–2026 about how motors are now being made, starting with L3Harris. No background needed. Each section starts in plain English and then gives the detail you need to hold your own in a conversation.

The 60-second version

  1. Demand exploded. Wars in Ukraine and the Middle East burned through interceptors and rockets. Every one needs at least one rocket motor, and for years only two U.S. companies made them: Northrop Grumman and L3Harris (which owns Aerojet Rocketdyne).
  2. The Pentagon started paying for factories directly. In January 2026 it agreed to invest $1 billion in L3Harris’s motor business, a first-of-its-kind deal. It followed with seven-year agreements to nearly triple PAC-3 and quadruple THAAD motor output.13
  3. The incumbents are rebuilding their factories. The new lines are consolidated, robot-assisted and “program-agnostic”, so they can switch between motor types instead of each line serving one missile.46
  4. New companies are betting on new processes. Anduril uses a bladeless mixer and flow-line production. Ursa Major 3D-prints the metal parts. X-Bow prints the propellant itself.91113
  5. Chemicals may be the real bottleneck. There is one U.S. source of the main oxidizer (ammonium perchlorate) and a thin supply of the standard binder. New factories only help if the ingredients keep up.16

What L3Harris has made public

L3Harris doesn’t publish its production process, but its press releases, contracts and state economic-development announcements say a lot about the direction. Here is the record in order.

  1. Feb 2025

    Camden, Arkansas groundbreaking

    New motor facilities funded under the Defense Production Act. Production of inert components, the non-explosive parts, shifts to Huntsville, Alabama so each site focuses on its core work.5

  2. May 2025

    Five “Factories of the Future” in Virginia

    The Orange County site gets modular factory design, robotic flex cells, “automated, progressive casting,” and a consolidated cast-and-assembly building that cuts motor travel by 90%.4

  3. Jul 2025

    Nearly $0.5B more for Camden

    Expansion of the Arkansas campus run by the Aerojet Rocketdyne subsidiary.4

  4. Nov 2025

    Arkansas Advanced Propulsion Facilities

    More than 20 buildings on 110 acres, aimed at six times the large-motor capacity. The facility adds 230,000 ft², taking the 2,000-acre site past 1.5 million ft² of manufacturing space. L3Harris says the equipment and buildings are “program-agnostic” and that it is automating with robotics for “repeatable, high quality, scalable, safe operations.”5

  5. Dec 2025

    First order through the new Camden plant

    GMLRS (guided rocket artillery) motors. The new facility raises annual capacity by more than 30%.5

  6. Jan 2026

    Pentagon invests $1 billion

    A convertible preferred investment from the Pentagon’s Industrial Base Analysis and Sustainment fund, in a Missile Solutions business L3Harris plans to take public in the second half of 2026. L3Harris keeps majority control. Programs named: PAC-3, THAAD, Tomahawk and Standard Missile. It is described as the first direct-to-supplier deal of its kind.1

  7. Apr 2026

    $1.2–1.3 billion Virginia campus

    An “advanced propulsion campus” in Orange County covering mixing, grinding, casting and final assembly, with dozens of new buildings, more than twice the manufacturing space and 350+ jobs. It reuses designs from the Arkansas “Factory of the Future.”6

  8. Jul 2026

    Seven-year production frameworks

    The first Pentagon deals built around rocket motors: nearly triple PAC-3 and quadruple THAAD output. The PAC-3 work covers the two-pulse motor, attitude control motors and the “Lethality Enhancer.”2

  9. Sep 2026

    $4.7 billion PAC-3 MSE propulsion award

    From Lockheed Martin, over seven years, and not yet finalized. Two new Camden facilities are due in 2027. The national goal is to raise PAC-3 MSE output from about 600 to 2,000 interceptors a year by 2030.3

Inference, not confirmed

How L3Harris is probably doing it

  • Standard building blocks, copied between sites. Virginia explicitly reuses Arkansas designs. Expect standard bay, mixer and cast-cell layouts rolled out to both states, which is what “modular factory” usually means.
  • Buildings not tied to one missile. “Program-agnostic” implies tooling and fixtures that change over between motor families, such as GMLRS, PAC-3 and Javelin-size motors, instead of a dedicated line for each program. Robotic flex cells are the usual way to do that.
  • Steady-flow casting instead of big batches. “Automated, progressive casting” most likely means motors moving through casting in a paced sequence, fed by mixing sized to match, rather than big batches carried between distant buildings. The 90% cut in travel supports that reading.
  • Specialized sites. Energetics (mixing, grinding, casting) are concentrated in Camden and Orange County, while non-explosive parts move to Huntsville. That keeps the heavily regulated work in fewer, purpose-built places.
  • A digital thread before new chemistry. L3Harris credits “digital process improvements” and new tooling for its near-term ramp. That points to better data and control of the existing cast process, rather than printing or new mixers, at least for current programs.

The playbook

Six ways factories are changing

1 · Put the whole flow under one roof

each step in its own building, with motors carried between them.

Consolidate the steps that handle live propellant into one building with adjoining bays, and move each motor through them one at a time (“one-piece flow”) instead of in big batches.

  • L3Harris: a single cast-and-assembly building, 90% less motor travel.4
  • Anduril: one-piece flow with parallel processes in Mississippi.9
  • Northrop: its 2026 Propulsion Innovation Center puts design, development, manufacturing and test on one campus.7
In plain English

Picture an assembly line in one building instead of a truck driving parts between nine buildings. That’s less handling, less waiting, and fewer chances to damage a motor.

Consolidation is really a safety-engineering problem, because you have to design around quantity-distance rules.

2 · Build factories that can switch products

a line built and certified for one missile program.

Use program-agnostic buildings, robotic flex cells and modular bays, so one line can change over between motor sizes as demand shifts.

  • L3Harris: “program-agnostic equipment and buildings.”5
  • Ursa Major: Lynx handles motor diameters from 2 to 22.5 inches without retooling.11
  • X-Bow: Rocket Factory in a Box, a containerized production cell that can be duplicated.14
In plain English

One flexible kitchen that can cook many dishes, instead of a dedicated kitchen for each dish.

Flex cells are how you survive demand swinging between programs.

3 · Mix faster, and in smaller amounts

huge bladed planetary mixers making very large batches over hours.

Bladeless mixers shake or spin sealed containers, so propellant mixes in minutes, in smaller and safer amounts, and batches can be matched to the casting rhythm.

  • Anduril and FlackTek: “Mega FlackTek,” a bladeless dual-asymmetric centrifugal mixer using 55-gallon drums. Anduril says mixing went from hours to minutes and expects more than ten times the throughput.10
  • Resonant acoustic mixing (RAM): another bladeless method using low-frequency vibration, which can in principle mix ingredients directly inside the case.10
In plain English

Instead of a giant dough mixer with paddles, the container itself is shaken or spun fast enough that everything blends with no blade touching it.

Bladeless mixing shrinks the batch and removes the blade, which is safer and faster to clean.

4 · 3D-print the propellant

pour the whole grain at once around a removable mandrel.

Extrude propellant paste through a print head and build the grain layer by layer, from a digital file.

  • X-Bow: patented AMSP (Additive Manufacturing of Solid Propellant) at Luling, Texas. It targets 1 million pounds of propellant a year initially and 3 million within 12–24 months. End-to-end live-energetics operations on two systems were announced in May 2026.1314
  • Backed by the Air Force Research Laboratory’s RE-ARM program.15
In plain English

A very large, very careful 3D printer that prints the fuel itself. See part 2 for the trade-offs.

Printing removes the mandrel, but qualifying a printed grain is the hard part.

5 · Print or re-source the hardware

long waits for machined cases, nozzles and igniters from a few suppliers.

Additively manufacture cases, nozzles and igniters in-house, or train adjacent suppliers to make them.

  • Ursa Major prints cases, nozzles and igniters. It says one machine can print more than 1,650 man-portable motor cases a year, against a Javelin surge rate of about 2,100.11
  • Materials Resources LLC: $25.2M from the Pentagon to show additively manufactured metal motor cases, 2026.17
  • Anduril taught a nozzle supplier to wind composite cases after spotting that it already had the winding machines.16
In plain English

The fuel is only half the motor. The metal and composite shell often has the longer lead time.

Case lead time can pace the whole motor, which is why vertical integration keeps coming up.

6 · Automate, measure, inspect with AI

manual handling, and people reviewing film X-rays by eye.

Robots handle and trim, the plant collects process data, and CT scans are screened by machine learning.

  • Northrop: the new Missile Integration Facility at Rocket Center, WV, is described as a “highly automated digital factory.”8
  • Anduril: robotics and digital analytics throughout production.9
  • Research: machine-learning defect detection on CT images of motors has reported about 98% classification accuracy, and CT digital twins were a 2026 conference topic.18
In plain English

Keep people away from the dangerous steps, and let software flag problems in scans so inspection doesn’t become the new bottleneck.

If you triple casting, you need CT and review that can keep up.

The catch

Supply-chain chokepoints

A motor is only as available as its scarcest ingredient. These are the weak links reporting kept pointing to in 2026. A red top means a single source today, and amber means a second source is coming.

Ammonium perchlorate

Oxidizer · sole U.S. source

American Pacific (AMPAC) is the only U.S. producer. A project of up to $100M adds a production line and more than 50% capacity, due around the end of 2026.1619

HTPB binder

Binder · second source coming

Helicon Chemical in Orlando is becoming a second supplier of HTPB-45M, but qualification takes 18–24 months.16

Heat-shield materials

Rayon precursor · new domestic source

ICF Mercantile received $9.3M to set up the first domestic source of the rayon precursor for carbon-phenolic nozzle and heat-shield materials.17

Safety devices

Arm-fire devices · expanding

PacSci EMC received $27.3M to build an integrated plant for the universal Arm Fire Device, the safety interlock that allows a motor to ignite.17

Bring it up

Things to ask or say

  • Is the Virginia campus a straight copy of the Arkansas Factory of the Future, or tuned for small and medium tactical motors?The April 2026 release says it reuses the Arkansas designs.
  • What does “program-agnostic” mean in practice: shared mixers and cast cells, or just shared buildings?This separates real flexibility from marketing.
  • Do the new entrants have a path to full-rate production, or are they still on prototype money?As of early 2026, Anduril, X-Bow and Ursa Major were mostly on development funding.
  • If PAC-3 goes from about 600 to 2,000 a year, is the limit motors, perchlorate or seekers?It shows you know the motor is only one chokepoint.
  • How do you qualify a bladeless-mixed or printed propellant against a heritage motor spec?Qualification is what turns a new process into a supplier.
  • Does the government stake in L3Harris’s Missile Solutions change how capacity gets prioritized?It’s the first equity deal of its kind, so the ground rules are new.
