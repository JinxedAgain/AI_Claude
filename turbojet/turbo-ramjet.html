      A ramjet gives no thrust standing still, so the turbofan does takeoff. As Mach rises the air reaches the compressor hotter and the compressor can add less, so the turbofan curves fall toward the ramjet's. Past about 700 K compressor inlet temperature (shaded) the turbomachinery has to be shut off and bypassed.

      What this model is. It is a representative design, not a specific production engine. It follows the turbine-based combined-cycle idea: a low-bypass afterburning turbofan with an outer ramjet duct. The translating spike and high-Mach bypass idea echo the SR-71's J58, which bled compressor air around the core into the afterburner. Proportions and blade counts are illustrative.

      Cycle model. Ideal gas with γ = 1.4. Efficiencies: fan 88%, compressor 86%, turbine 90%. Burner pressure retention 0.95 for the main burner and 0.94 for the afterburner. Inlet recovery follows MIL-E-5008B. Nozzle fully expanded. Fuel heating value 42.8 MJ/kg. Limits: turbine inlet 1,750 K, compressor exit 950 K, afterburner and ramjet 2,150 K. Climb path: sea level at Mach 0, 11 km by Mach 1.2, 20 km by Mach 3, then higher. Numbers are for teaching trends, not performance claims.
